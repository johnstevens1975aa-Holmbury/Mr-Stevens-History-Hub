⛓️ Slavery & the Slave Trade

This topic now starts before the Atlantic slave trade, so students understand West Africa as a region with powerful societies, urban centres, trade networks, scholarship, art and government before European demand expanded the traffic in enslaved people.

🌍 Life in West Africa before slavery

West Africa was not an empty or undeveloped place. Kingdoms and empires such as Mali, Songhai and Benin were linked by trade routes, ruled complex cities, produced skilled art and metalwork, and supported learning in places such as Timbuktu.

Activity 1 — Challenge the myth
  1. Write three facts proving West Africa had advanced societies before Atlantic slavery.
  2. Explain why starting the topic with Benin, Mali and Timbuktu changes how students understand the slave trade.
  3. Platinum: Which evidence is stronger — maps, objects or written sources? Justify your answer.

🔺 Triangular Trade — labelled map

Activity 2 — Map the system
  1. Copy the three arrows and label what was traded on each leg.
  2. Explain who gained profit at each stage and who suffered harm.
  3. Challenge: why does a neat triangle make the system look simpler than the human reality?

🚢 The Middle Passage — source evidence

WACA source task

Who? Abolitionists. Audience? British public and Parliament. Context? campaign against the slave trade. Aim? to shock people into action. Write a utility judgement.

Key people: Olaudah Equiano, Mary Prince, William Wilberforce, Thomas Clarkson.
Key dates: 1787 Brookes diagram, 1789 Equiano, 1807 trade abolished, 1833 slavery abolished.

🏭 Farms to Factories

The factory topic keeps the same simple visual pattern: one strong topic image, then supporting source images where they directly help the activity.

Activity — Domestic to factory

Explain two changes from the domestic system to the factory system. Then judge whether the biggest change was speed, discipline, technology or living conditions.

🏙️ Victorian Living Conditions

The public-health visuals now prioritise evidence students can actually discuss: overcrowding, Snow’s map and Bazalgette’s sewer construction.

Activity — Why did reform happen?
  1. Use the three images to explain why city conditions became impossible to ignore.
  2. Explain how Snow, Chadwick and Bazalgette each contributed a different kind of evidence or solution.
  3. Judgement: was reform caused more by science, government action, engineering or public pressure?
Key reforms: Chadwick Report 1842, Broad Street pump 1854, Great Stink 1858, Bazalgette sewers, Public Health Act 1875.
Impact: cleaner water, proper sewers, fewer cholera outbreaks, stronger government responsibility for public health.

🌍 The British Empire

This section keeps Empire images specific: map evidence for scale, India evidence for the Raj, and key people where the image supports the paragraph.

Activity — Two perspectives

Write one paragraph explaining claimed benefits of empire and one paragraph explaining harm. Use India as your example, then finish with a balanced judgement.
